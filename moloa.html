01 / Background & research question

Risk does not stop at loan approval.

Conventional credit modelling can separate the origination score, subsequent payment monitoring and treatment of distressed accounts. MOLOA investigates whether coordinating these stages makes credit decisions more interpretable and keeps the earlier signal useful later. Its specific problems are opaque aggregate risk scores, disconnected lifecycle models and selection bias in observational loan-modification studies.

PROBLEM 01

Understand the decision

Instead of one opaque default score, Phase 1 separates risk signals across five domain-oriented agents and models disagreement before routing or escalation. The aim is to reveal where assessments conflict, not merely improve headline accuracy.

PROBLEM 02

Keep lifecycle context

Origination risk may remain informative when a loan reaches month six. Phase 2 explicitly carries the routed origination probability into a survival model rather than discarding the first-stage output.

PROBLEM 03

Question treatment effects

Distressed loans receiving modification are not randomly selected. Phase 3 uses propensity-based weighting and a T-learner to examine intervention outcomes, rather than equating an observational difference with a randomized causal effect.

02 / Architecture

One loan lifecycle, three analytical stages.

The pipeline operates at three decision points: origination (month 0), early monitoring (month 6) and the first 60-plus-days-past-due event. It combines separately evaluated predictive and causal components, rather than being an LLM-agent orchestration system.

03 / Technical and design scrutiny

Why this design, and where it is fragile.

ROUTING

Separate experts, learn to combine

The Learned Agent Router uses entropy-regularised attention across five risk-domain agents to discourage collapse onto a single expert. The Conflict Arbitration Network models agent disagreement, and Adaptive Escalation Logic decides whether extra review is warranted. The advantage is analyzable disagreement; the cost is complexity and additional model validation.

TEMPORAL

Survival analysis, not another snapshot

A Cox proportional-hazards model captures time-to-event information. The system carries origination risk forward and adds KalmanHD trajectory features from ongoing performance. High concordance is evidence of ranking ability on this dataset, but not proof of calibrated future default probabilities.

CAUSAL

Adjustment is not randomization

IPTW estimates the average association after weighting by estimated treatment propensity, while a T-learner explores heterogeneous effects. The results depend on measured-confounder adequacy, treatment overlap and model specification; unmeasured confounding can still undermine a causal interpretation.

04 / Reproducible evidence

50,000 loans. Real trade-offs, not only wins.

The project README reports 50,000 loans from the 2022 Freddie Mac sample, a 1.82% default prevalence, an 80/20 stratified split and five-fold cross-validation. Figures below are repository-reported research results, not externally verified deployment metrics.

PHASE 1

Imbalanced classification

Macro F1: 0.5535; default-class F1: 0.1279; AUPRC: 0.0793. MOLOA leads the benchmark on reported imbalance-sensitive metrics, but its cross-validated ROC-AUC of 0.7408 trails logistic regression at 0.7928. The low minority-class F1 also shows substantial error remains.

PHASE 2

Month-six monitoring

Reported Cox concordance is 0.9707 ± 0.0026 across five folds; Phase 1 and Phase 2 agree on 96.91% of month-six loans. These values describe this evaluation setup, not a guaranteed performance level in a different population or period.

PHASE 3

Modification outcomes

IPTW estimates a +25.3 percentage-point six-month cure effect with a reported 95% bootstrap interval of [+17.3, +31.7]. This is an observational estimate that still requires sensitivity analysis and external validation before informing real loan-modification policy.

05 / Demonstration & inspectability

See the model outputs without overstating the demo.

The interactive browser demo shows the five agents, decision stages, prediction-based examples and a what-if scorer. Its adjustable what-if results use a calibrated approximation of the trained models; the committed Phase 1–3 prediction files support tracing selected real sample loans. The notebook documents how to rebuild the full pipeline from the separately licensed source data.

DEMO

Scenario exploration

Change FICO, debt-to-income ratio, loan-to-value and delinquency signals to inspect how the demonstrator changes its outcome. The UI is useful for explaining decisions, not a real underwriting service.

EVIDENCE

Full loan trace

demo_full.py reads committed prediction files to follow a sample mortgage through the phases. These outputs differ from the lightweight hypothetical scorer and should not be conflated.

REPRODUCE

Notebook and results

The project includes a full pipeline notebook, benchmark outputs, prediction files and reproducibility guidance. The raw Freddie Mac loan data and full model pickles are not redistributed.

06 / Impact & limitations

Research evidence, not a live lending decision system.

What the project contributes

The practical research contribution is a traceable framework that links origination, ongoing risk monitoring and treatment-effect estimation. It supports investigation of agent disagreement and the role of lifecycle signals. No measured reduction in lender losses, operational expense or default rates is claimed.

What remains open

  • External validation across vintages, lenders and changed economic conditions
  • Calibration and fairness testing across borrower groups, with policy and regulatory review
  • Sensitivity tests for unmeasured confounding and treatment-selection bias
  • Live deployment, human oversight, monitoring and controlled evaluation before real-world decision support

The results above summarize the reported research evaluation. Explore the interactive research demo; it is not a production underwriting tool.
